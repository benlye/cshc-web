{% include 'blocks/_middle_heading.html' with title="Complaints and Concerns" %}

Cambridge South Hockey Club aims to provide a positive experience for everyone involved in the Club. We recognise, however, that concerns or complaints may sometimes arise.

We encourage members to raise concerns so that they can be addressed fairly, promptly and at an appropriate level.

{% include 'blocks/_middle_subheading.html' with title="Raising a Concern" %}

Where appropriate, concerns should initially be raised informally with the person best placed to resolve them, such as a captain, coach or Club officer.

Many issues can be resolved quickly through a conversation and we encourage members to use this approach where they feel comfortable doing so.

There is no requirement to attempt informal resolution where the matter is serious, involves safeguarding or welfare, or where the person raising the concern does not consider an informal approach appropriate.

{% include 'blocks/_middle_subheading.html' with title="Formal Complaints" %}

Where a concern cannot be resolved informally, or where the nature of the matter makes an informal approach inappropriate, a formal complaint may be made to the {% clubinfo_email 'SecretaryEmail' secretary_name %}.

A formal complaint should normally be made in writing and should explain:

  • what has happened
  • when and, where relevant, where it happened
  • who was involved
  • any steps already taken to resolve the matter
  • any relevant supporting information
  • what outcome or resolution the complainant is seeking

Complaints should be raised as soon as reasonably practicable so that the circumstances can be properly considered.

{% include 'blocks/_middle_subheading.html' with title="How We Will Deal with a Complaint" %}

The Club will acknowledge a formal complaint and identify an appropriate person or group to consider it.

The Club will:

  • consider the complaint fairly and impartially
  • ensure that anyone handling the complaint does not have a material conflict of interest
  • give anyone whose conduct is the subject of a complaint a reasonable opportunity to understand and respond to the concerns raised
  • seek further information where necessary
  • handle information sensitively and share it only where reasonably necessary to consider the matter
  • communicate the outcome to the complainant when the process is complete

The procedure followed will be proportionate to the nature and seriousness of the complaint. Some matters may be resolved through discussion or mediation, while others may require a more formal investigation or consideration by members of the Club Committee.

Where a complaint identifies an opportunity to improve Club policies or practices, the Club will consider appropriate changes.

{% include 'blocks/_middle_subheading.html' with title="Conflicts of Interest" %}

A person who is directly involved in a complaint, or who has another material conflict of interest, should not be responsible for deciding its outcome.

If a complaint concerns the Club Secretary, it should be raised with the Chair. If it concerns the Chair, it should be raised with another appropriate member of the Club Committee.

The Club may appoint other suitably independent people to consider a complaint where this is necessary to ensure that it is handled fairly.

{% include 'blocks/_middle_subheading.html' with title="Outcome and Review" %}

The complainant will be informed of the outcome of a formal complaint and, where appropriate, any action the Club proposes to take.

Where the complainant believes that the complaint has not been handled fairly or that significant relevant information has not been considered, they may ask for the outcome to be reviewed.

A review should, wherever practicable, be undertaken by someone who was not responsible for the original decision.

A review is not normally a complete rehearing of the complaint, but will consider whether the complaint was handled fairly, whether relevant information was properly considered, and whether the outcome was reasonable in the circumstances.

{% include 'blocks/_middle_subheading.html' with title="Safeguarding Concerns" %}

A concern involving the welfare or safeguarding of a child or young person should be reported promptly to the Club Welfare Officer and will be handled in accordance with the Club's Safeguarding Policy and England Hockey's safeguarding procedures.

Safeguarding concerns should not be investigated informally by members of the Club before being referred through the appropriate safeguarding process.

{% include 'blocks/_middle_subheading.html' with title="Behaviour and Disciplinary Matters" %}

Concerns about behaviour may also fall within the Club's Behaviour Policy and Code of Conduct or England Hockey's Spirit of Hockey – Code of Conduct and Disciplinary Regulations.

Where appropriate, the Club may deal with a matter under its disciplinary procedures or refer it to England Hockey or the relevant hockey authority.

England Hockey imposes time limits on some disciplinary and misconduct complaints. Anyone considering making a complaint directly to England Hockey should therefore consult the current England Hockey procedures promptly.

{% include 'blocks/_middle_subheading.html' with title="Serious Concerns and Whistleblowing" %}

Members are encouraged to raise genuine concerns about serious wrongdoing, unsafe practices, safeguarding failures or conduct which may place individuals or the Club at risk.

Where the concern involves those who would normally receive a complaint, it may be raised directly with another appropriate Club officer or the Chair.

Where it would not be appropriate to raise the matter within the Club, concerns may be raised directly with England Hockey or another appropriate authority.

No member should be disadvantaged for raising a genuine concern in good faith or for supporting someone else who does so.

{% include 'blocks/_middle_subheading.html' with title="Confidentiality" %}

Complaints and concerns will be handled as sensitively and confidentially as reasonably possible.

Absolute confidentiality cannot always be guaranteed. Information may need to be shared with the person complained about, witnesses, Club officers, England Hockey or another appropriate authority in order to investigate or respond to a concern fairly.

Where possible, the Club will explain when information needs to be shared and why.

{% include 'blocks/_middle_subheading.html' with title="Appropriate Use of the Complaints Process" %}

Everyone involved in a complaint is expected to communicate respectfully and cooperate reasonably with the process.

The Club may decline to reconsider a matter which has already been properly considered unless significant new information is provided.

The Club may also take appropriate action where a complaint is knowingly false, malicious or forms part of a pattern of abusive or unreasonable behaviour. Making a complaint which is not upheld does not, by itself, mean that the complaint was made maliciously.
